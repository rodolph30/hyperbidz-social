# HyperBidz "Did You Know?" Research Bank: Auctions in Lebanon, Markets & the World

*Research for a daily series of 60-second Instagram explainers. Compiled 16 October 2026.*

**Series goals, and the tags used on every item**
- **TEACH**: explain how auctions work.
- **AWARE**: show that auctions already exist in Lebanon (court, bank, state, municipal), but they are opaque, slow, paper-based and hard for ordinary people to join.
- **NEED**: make viewers feel Lebanon needs an open, modern auction marketplace.

**Confidence labels**
- **Verified**: confirmed in a primary/official source, or in two or more reputable outlets.
- **Partly verified**: the core fact is sourced, but a detail (exact figure, outcome or date) rests on one secondary source, or the wording needs care.
- **Unverified / dropped**: listed at the end so nobody scripts it by mistake.

**About the Lebanese law quoted here.** Article numbers for the Lebanese Code of Civil Procedure (قانون أصول المحاكمات المدنية, Decree-Law 90/1983, "CCP") come from the Lebanese University Legal Informatics Center (legiliban.ul.edu.lb). That database marks amended articles as "معدلة". Two cautions:
1. Lira amounts written into the code (for example LBP 1,000,000) may have been changed by later budget or fee laws. Check before quoting a lira threshold on screen.
2. Have a Lebanese lawyer review any episode that gives exact day-counts. Section 0 notes one apparent overlap, between Art. 978 and Art. 983.

## 0. Cheat sheet: how a Lebanese court property auction actually works (CCP, Book on Execution)

Source for every step: CCP Arts. 948–987 as published at legiliban.ul.edu.lb ([seizure & valuation, Arts. 948–964](http://legiliban.ul.edu.lb/LawArticles.aspx?LawTreeSectionID=260006&lawId=244565), [conditions of sale, Arts. 965–968](http://legiliban.ul.edu.lb/LawArticles.aspx?LawTreeSectionID=260007&lawId=244565), [auction session, Arts. 969–987](http://legiliban.ul.edu.lb/LawArticles.aspx?LawTreeSectionID=260008&lawId=244565)). The Execution Department (دائرة التنفيذ) sits beside each first-instance court and is presided over by a single judge (القاضي المنفرد) (Art. 827, [source](http://77.42.251.205/LawArticles.aspx?LawID=244565&LawTreeSectionID=259987&language=ar)).

| Step | What happens | Article |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Trigger | A creditor holding an enforceable title (typically a bank with a mortgage, or any judgment creditor) files an execution request with the land-registry extract. | 948 |
| 2. Warning | The execution officer (مأمور التنفيذ) warns the debtor to pay **within 5 days**. | 950 (amended) |
| 3. Seizure | If the debtor doesn't pay, the judge orders the seizure, which is entered in the land register. From registration, the seizing creditor holds a real right on the property. | 952, 958 |
| 4. Expert valuation | Within 5 days of the registry's reply, the judge appoints an **expert**. The expert visits the property with the execution officer and the mukhtar (or two local witnesses) to describe it and **estimate its value**. | 954 (amended) |
| 5. Conditions of sale (دفتر الشروط) | Drafted within 8 days. **Starting price (بدل الطرح) = six-tenths (60%) of the estimated value.** | 965 |
| 6. Objections | Interested parties get 5 days to comment on or object to the conditions book. If more than **2 years** pass between valuation and auction, the judge may order a new valuation. | 966–968 |
| 7. Publicity | Notice in **two daily newspapers + the Official Gazette**, posted on the Execution Department board, the debtor's home, the property's door, **the municipality's door (or the mukhtar's house)** and **the town square**, at least **10 days** before the session. The article does not mention the internet. | 969–971 |
| 8. Deposit to bid | Every bidder must first deposit, in the judge's name at the Treasury or an approved bank, **an amount equal to the full starting price**, or provide a bank guarantee for it. | 973 (amended) |
| 9. Creditor exemption | A creditor whose debt is at least the starting price is **exempt from the deposit** (if no higher-ranking creditor competes). | 974 |
| 10. The session | Held publicly **before the Execution Department head**, at the department or another place the judge chooses. The crier (المباشر) calls out the starting price. *"Every higher bid, even an invalid one, cancels the bid before it."* | 975 (amended) |
| 11. Hammer | The judge awards the property to the last, highest bidder **once fifteen minutes have passed** (to the earliest offer if bids are tied). | 976 |
| 12. No bidders | The sale is postponed and **the starting price is reduced**, then re-advertised. The judge **repeats this until a buyer appears**. (The published text reads "postponed for a period ranging between two months…", which looks truncated online.) | 976 |
| 13. Losers | Unsuccessful bidders take back their deposits as soon as the session ends. | 977 |
| 14. The Lebanese "10% rule" (زيادة العشر) | If the winner does **not deposit the full price within 3 days** of the award, a new session is advertised. At that session **anyone may take over with a 10% increase**, provided they bring the full price plus the increase (or a bank guarantee). If nobody does, the original buyer can still pay in that session. Otherwise the property is re-auctioned at his risk and he owes any shortfall. | 978 |
| 15. Paying | After the award, the buyer must pay price, fees and costs **within 20 days** and is treated as owner. Eviction of the debtor can follow 5 days after notice. *(Art. 978's 3-day deposit and Art. 983's 20-day payment overlap; check with a lawyer before scripting day-counts.)* | 983 (amended) |
| 16. Debtor's last chance | Within **10 days** of the award the debtor can deposit enough to pay all debts and costs. The award is then cancelled. | 984 |
| 17. Appeal | The award decision can be appealed **within 5 days** (procedure defects only). | 987 (amended) |
| 18. Registration | The award is registered in the land register, which transfers ownership and wipes the mortgages. | 985–986 |
| Delays allowed | The judge may postpone **2–6 months** "taking into account the debtor's situation and the economic situation" (if the debtor advances interest). He must postpone **one year** if the debtor proves his property's income will pay everyone. | 979 (amended), 980 |

**Movable property (cars, goods, jewellery), CCP Arts. 921–936** ([source](http://legiliban.ul.edu.lb/LawArticles.aspx?LawTreeSectionID=260002&lawId=244565)):
- The sale must be ordered within 5 days of the seizure report (Art. 921).
- Advertised 8 to 15 days ahead in daily papers, the Official Gazette, posters in the town square and the court board (Art. 924). Posters alone are enough if the value does not exceed **LBP 1,000,000** (Art. 925).
- An item cannot be sold below **60%** of its estimated value (Art. 928). **Gold, silver, jewellery and antiques** cannot be sold below **90%** (Art. 929).
- The officer may use **"the municipality's crier" (دلال البلدية)** or another auctioneer (Art. 930).
- If there are no bidders: reduce the price, postpone 15–30 days, and repeat until someone buys (Art. 931).
- The price is paid **on the spot** (Art. 932).
- Perishables can be sold "from one hour to the next" (Art. 923).

## A. LEBANON

### L1. You need to deposit the full opening price just to raise your hand
- **Hook:** Did you know that to bid in a Lebanese court auction you must first lock up money equal to the whole opening price?
- **Details:** Opening price (بدل الطرح) = 60% of the expert valuation (CCP Art. 965). Each bidder must deposit **100% of that opening price**, in the judge's name at the Treasury or an approved bank, or bring a bank guarantee, *before* bidding starts (Art. 973). On a flat valued at $200,000, that means parking about **$120,000** just to enter the room. *(Illustrative arithmetic.)*
- **Why it matters:** **NEED / AWARE.** A huge entry barrier keeps ordinary people out and leaves the field to the well-capitalised.
- **60-sec angle:** Open on a "$120,000 to say *I bid*" caption. Walk through valuation → 60% → 100% deposit. Close: imagine verified bidders with smart deposits instead.
- **Sources:** CCP Arts. 965, 973 ([legiliban 260007](http://legiliban.ul.edu.lb/LawArticles.aspx?LawTreeSectionID=260007&lawId=244565), [legiliban 260008](http://legiliban.ul.edu.lb/LawArticles.aspx?LawTreeSectionID=260008&lawId=244565)).
- **Confidence:** Verified (law text). The dollar example is illustrative.

### L2. The bank can bid without paying a deposit
- **Hook:** Did you know the creditor (often the bank foreclosing on you) can bid at your auction without the deposit everyone else must pay?
- **Details:** Art. 974 exempts a creditor whose debt is at least the opening price from the deposit or guarantee, as long as no higher-ranking creditor competes. If the debt is smaller, the creditor deposits only the difference.
- **Why it matters:** **AWARE.** The playing field isn't level.
- **60-sec angle:** Split screen: "you" queue at the bank to deposit; "the bank" walks straight in. Explain Art. 974.
- **Source:** CCP Art. 974 ([legiliban 260008](http://legiliban.ul.edu.lb/LawArticles.aspx?LawTreeSectionID=260008&lawId=244565)).
- **Confidence:** Verified (law text). Don't claim how often banks actually do this; no data found.

### L3. Lebanese court auctions are advertised on paper, on walls and on the municipality door
- **Hook:** Did you know the law announces a Lebanese property auction in two newspapers, the Official Gazette and a poster on the town-hall door, and doesn't mention the internet?
- **Details:** The judge orders publication in **two daily newspapers he chooses + the Official Gazette**. The notice is also posted on the Execution Department board, the debtor's home, the property's door, the municipality door (or the mukhtar's house) and the town square, **at least 10 days** before the session (Arts. 970–971). Proof of posting is a certificate from the officer or the mukhtar (Art. 972).
- **Why it matters:** **AWARE / NEED.** Visibility is the whole point of an auction, and here visibility is paper-based and local.
- **60-sec angle:** "Where would you look for a court auction tomorrow?" Montage of a newspaper, a gazette, a lamp-post poster. Then a phone notification.
- **Source:** CCP Arts. 969–972 ([legiliban 260008](http://legiliban.ul.edu.lb/LawArticles.aspx?LawTreeSectionID=260008&lawId=244565)).
- **Confidence:** Verified.

### L4. The 15-minute hammer, and "every bid, even an invalid one, kills the one before"
- **Hook:** Did you know Lebanese law says a court auction ends after fifteen minutes?
- **Details:** The session is public, before the Execution Department head. The crier calls out the opening price, and *"كل زيادة ولو باطلة تسقط الزيادة التي سبقتها"*: every higher bid, even an invalid one, cancels the previous one (Art. 975). The judge awards the property to the last highest bidder "after fifteen minutes have passed", or to the earliest offer if bids are tied (Art. 976).
- **Why it matters:** **TEACH.** It explains the English (ascending) auction format with a local, legal twist.
- **60-sec angle:** A 15-minute countdown on screen while explaining English auctions, then the "invalid bid" quirk.
- **Source:** CCP Arts. 975–976 ([legiliban 260008](http://legiliban.ul.edu.lb/LawArticles.aspx?LawTreeSectionID=260008&lawId=244565)).
- **Confidence:** Verified (law text). Say "the law sets fifteen minutes"; don't claim how courtrooms run it in practice.

### L5. زيادة العشر: the Lebanese 10% rule
- **Hook:** Did you know you can "steal" a Lebanese court auction by adding 10%, but only if the winner fails to pay?
- **Details:** If the winning bidder doesn't deposit the full price **within 3 days** of the award, the judge sets a **new session** that names the property, the first price and the first buyer. At that session, anyone who **accepts the price plus one-tenth (10%)** and brings the full amount (or an equivalent bank guarantee) restarts the bidding at that level. If no one does, the first buyer can still pay, with late costs, in that same session. Otherwise the property is re-auctioned at his risk and he owes any price shortfall (CCP Art. 978). **Nuance:** in Lebanon this 10% overbid is tied to the defaulting buyer. It differs from France's general *surenchère du dixième*, where anyone may outbid any sale by 10% ([Légifrance R322-50](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/section_lc/LEGITEXT000025024948/LEGISCTA000025939177)).
- **Why it matters:** **TEACH / AWARE.** It's a real local rule almost nobody knows.
- **60-sec angle:** "Winner didn't pay in 3 days? Here's how a stranger takes the house with +10%." Simple timeline graphic.
- **Sources:** CCP Art. 978 ([legiliban 260008](http://legiliban.ul.edu.lb/LawArticles.aspx?LawTreeSectionID=260008&lawId=244565)). French comparison: Légifrance.
- **Confidence:** Verified (law text). Lawyer check advised on the interplay with Art. 983 (20 days to pay price, fees and costs).

### L6. If nobody bids, the price drops, and drops again
- **Hook:** Did you know that if nobody bids on a seized property in Lebanon, the judge must lower the price and try again until somebody buys?
- **Details:** With no buyer, the sale is postponed, **the opening price is reduced**, and it's re-advertised. The judge "must repeat this until a buyer comes forward" (Art. 976). Movables follow the same logic: reduce, postpone 15–30 days, repeat (Art. 931). Movables can't go below 60% of their valuation (Art. 928), jewellery and antiques below 90% (Art. 929). If a property valuation is more than 2 years old, it may be redone (Art. 968).
- **Why it matters:** **TEACH / AWARE.** A built-in "descending" mechanism, which is a Dutch auction in slow motion, and a source of bargains that few people ever see.
- **60-sec angle:** A price tag shrinking on each new newspaper notice. "Who is watching these? Usually not you."
- **Source:** CCP Arts. 928–931, 968, 976 ([legiliban](http://legiliban.ul.edu.lb/LawArticles.aspx?LawTreeSectionID=260002&lawId=244565)).
- **Confidence:** Verified (law text). The size of each reduction is left to the judge for real estate.

### L7. The debtor's 10-day lifeline, and the judge's power to pause for "the economic situation"
- **Hook:** Did you know a Lebanese owner can cancel the auction of their own home after the hammer falls?
- **Details:** Within **10 days** of the award, the debtor can deposit enough to pay all debts and costs. The award is then cancelled and the seizure lifted (Art. 984). Before that, the judge may postpone the sale **2–6 months**, taking "the debtor's position and the economic situation" into account, if the debtor advances interest (Art. 979). The judge must postpone **one year** if the debtor proves the net income of his property will pay all creditors (Art. 980). The award can be appealed within 5 days (Art. 987).
- **Why it matters:** **TEACH / AWARE.** It explains why court auctions are slow and uncertain for buyers.
- **60-sec angle:** "You won! …or did you?" Countdown of 3 days / 5 days / 10 days / 20 days.
- **Source:** CCP Arts. 979, 980, 984, 987 ([legiliban 260008](http://legiliban.ul.edu.lb/LawArticles.aspx?LawTreeSectionID=260008&lawId=244565)).
- **Confidence:** Verified (law text).

### L8. Lebanese law still mentions "the municipality's crier"
- **Hook:** Did you know Lebanon's civil procedure code still lets court officers hire "the municipality's crier" (دلال البلدية) to auction seized goods?
- **Details:** For movables, the execution officer runs the bidding and awards to the highest bidder. He may use the municipal crier or another crier he picks, whose fee comes out of the sale. He records every bidder's name and price in minutes (CCP Art. 930). The price is due immediately (Art. 932).
- **Why it matters:** **TEACH / AWARE.** It links the traditional souk *dallal* to the formal legal system.
- **60-sec angle:** Old-souk *dallal* voice-over leading into the actual article text.
- **Source:** CCP Art. 930 ([legiliban 260002](http://legiliban.ul.edu.lb/LawArticles.aspx?LawTreeSectionID=260002&lawId=244565)).
- **Confidence:** Verified.

### L9. A whole lira threshold that's now worth about $11
- **Hook:** Did you know Lebanese law lets a court skip newspaper ads if the seized goods are worth under one million lira, which today is roughly $11?
- **Details:** Art. 925 (amended) allows posters only when the value doesn't exceed LBP 1,000,000. Art. 928 lets the officer's own estimate stand if it is LBP 160,000. At BDL's LBP 89,500/$ rate, those are about **$11** and **$1.8**. *(Our conversion.)*
- **Why it matters:** **AWARE / NEED.** The rules didn't keep up with the crisis.
- **60-sec angle:** Show 1,000,000 LBP in cash, then "= one manouche combo". Pivot to rules frozen in time.
- **Sources:** CCP Arts. 925, 928 ([legiliban 260002](http://legiliban.ul.edu.lb/LawArticles.aspx?LawTreeSectionID=260002&lawId=244565)); BDL rate series showing 89,500 ([BDL](https://bdl.gov.lb/economicandfinancialdatasub.php?code=9&docId=161&filecode=90)).
- **Confidence:** Partly verified. The text is as published on legiliban; check that no later fee or budget law updated these amounts.

### L10. Lebanon froze legal deadlines for years after 2019, auctions included
- **Hook:** Did you know Lebanon passed law after law that froze legal, judicial and contractual deadlines, including loan payments and court procedures?
- **Details:**
  - **Law 160 of 8 May 2020** suspended legal, judicial and contractual deadlines.
  - **Law 185 of 19 Aug 2020** extended this and also suspended contractual deadlines "including those for the payment of loans and dues, as well as ongoing procedures" for 6 months.
  - **Law 199 of 29 Dec 2020** extended it again to 30 June 2021. Laws 194/2020 and 212/2021 also extended deadlines.
  - **Law 328 of 4 Dec 2024** suspended legal and contractual deadlines from 8 Oct 2023 to 31 Mar 2025, and judicial deadlines for an earlier period. On **16 Jan 2025** the Constitutional Council annulled the judicial-deadlines paragraph, Art. 1(2).
- **Why it matters:** **AWARE.** It explains why foreclosure auctions stalled.
- **60-sec angle:** "The pause button on Lebanese justice." A timeline of freezing laws.
- **Sources:** [Aldic on Laws 160/185](https://www.aldic.net/category/laws-decrees/page/2), [Aldic Law 185](https://www.aldic.net/law-no-185-of-19082020-relating-to-the-prorogation-of-the-suspension-of-legal-judicial-and-contractual-deadlines/), [Aldic Law 199](https://www.aldic.net/law-no-199-of-29122020-relating-to-the-prorogation-of-the-suspension-of-legal-judicial-and-contractual-deadlines), [NRC report (194/2020, 212/2021)](https://www.nrc.no/globalassets/pdf/reports/hlp-in-beirut/hlp-in-beirut-in-the-light-of-the-port-blast-full-report.pdf), [Chambers on Law 328/2024](https://chambers.com/content/item/6191).
- **Confidence:** Verified. Don't claim auctions were "banned"; the laws suspended deadlines.

### L11. Judges went on strike: 450 of 560 stopped working
- **Hook:** Did you know that in 2022 most of Lebanon's judges stopped working for about five months?
- **Details:** An open-ended judges' strike began in **August 2022**. By late November 2022, **more than 450 of Lebanon's 560 judges** had stopped working (Arab News). Courts were paralysed, and depositors' lawsuits against banks were suspended (The Media Line). Judges returned after about **five months** (Waradana, Jan 2023).
- **Why it matters:** **AWARE / NEED.** When the only auction channel is a courtroom, a strike stops the market.
- **60-sec angle:** An empty courtroom photo with "Auction postponed" stamps.
- **Sources:** [Arab News 28 Nov 2022](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2207781/middle-east), [The Media Line](https://themedialine.org/by-region/lebanons-judiciary-languishes-due-to-lack-of-independence), [Waradana](https://waradana.com/english/article/168952-lebanon-faces-concealed-judicial-paralysis-overcrowding-rush-and-delays).
- **Confidence:** Verified.

### L12. "Lollar" repayments: why the foreclosure wave never came
- **Hook:** Did you know 609,896 Lebanese borrowers repaid about $58.5 billion of loans after 2019, mostly below their real value?
- **Details:** Per BDL figures reported by L'Orient Today, **609,896 borrowers** repaid the equivalent of **$58.5 billion** in loans between Oct 2019 and Apr 2024. **89.4%** of that was repaid at prices lower than the initial value (lollars or lira at old rates). Banks stopped lending.
- **Why it matters:** **AWARE.** Many feared mass bank foreclosure auctions; instead, debts were cleared with devalued money.
- **60-sec angle:** "The auction that never happened": $100 of debt paid with a $13 cheque.
- **Source:** [L'Orient Today, 1 Jul 2024](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1418921/economic-news-recap-heres-what-happened-last-week-in-lebanon-18.html).
- **Confidence:** Verified (BDL data via L'Orient Today).

### L13. Property bought with bank cheques: 71% of post-crisis sales happened in three years
- **Hook:** Did you know that most Lebanese property sales after the 2019 crash were paid with trapped bank cheques?
- **Details:** Al-Sifr's analysis: **71%** of property sales in 2020–2025 took place in **2020–2022**, representing **84.2%** of the total sales value. Depositors used bank cheques, counted at face value, to escape trapped deposits. Sales then fell in 2023–2024, partly because land-registry offices were closed by strikes.
- **Why it matters:** **AWARE.** It shows how prices get distorted when there is no transparent price discovery.
- **60-sec angle:** "A cheque worth 13 cents on the dollar bought a whole apartment."
- **Source:** [Al-Sifr](https://alsifr.org/real-estate-sector-developments-lebanon).
- **Confidence:** Verified (single specialist source, data-driven).

### L14. A depositor used the Execution Department to get every dollar back, a first
- **Hook:** Did you know that in July 2026 a depositor used enforcement proceedings to force a Lebanese bank to repay his deposit in full, in fresh dollars, for the first time?

### L15. Banks must sell the property they take
- **Hook:** Did you know Lebanese banks aren't allowed to just keep the real estate they acquire from bad debts?

It was updated by Intermediate Circulars **567 (26 Aug 2020)** and **637 (27 Jul 2022)**.
- **Why it matters:** **AWARE / NEED.** Bank-owned property is a natural supply for a transparent marketplace.
- **60-sec angle:** "Where do repossessed homes go?"
- **Sources:** [BDL basic circulars list](https://www.banqueduliban.gov.lb/basiccirculars.php?langid=EN&page=7&tpages=12), [BDL Intermediate 567](https://www.bdl.gov.lb/intermediatecirc_detail.php?id=3971&langid=EN), [BDL Intermediate 637 listing](https://www.banqueduliban.gov.lb/intermediatecirculars.php?catid=59&langid=EN).
- **Confidence:** Partly verified. The circulars are confirmed, but the exact deadline (often cited as 2 years) was not confirmed in the text, so don't quote a deadline.

  | 666 Y | 45,000,000 |
  | 1111 M | 27,000,000 |
  | 77777 Y | 20,000,000 |
  | 555555 Y | 13,500,000 |

  Rules and process:

- **Confidence:** Verified for the process and starting prices. **Final hammer prices were not found.** Don't quote any.

### L18. The Lebanese state auctions almost everything: olive harvests, a weekly souk, one used Mitsubishi
- **Hook:** Did you know Lebanese municipalities auction the right to pick a season's olives?
- **Details:** Examples on the Public Procurement Authority portal (method: "Auction"):

## B. STOCKS & MARKETS (bidding meets finance)

### S1. A stock exchange is one giant, never-ending auction
- **Hook:** Did you know every stock price you see is the result of an auction that runs all day?
- **Details:** Modern exchanges run a **continuous double auction**. Buyers post bids, sellers post asks, and a **limit order book** matches them in real time. The stock exchange is the textbook example of a double auction. The Beirut Stock Exchange's own guide tells investors to place buy or sell orders "specifying… the price you are ready to pay or get", within the exchange's price thresholds.
- **Why it matters:** **TEACH.** It bridges auctions and investing.
- **60-sec angle:** Animated order book: bids left, asks right, a trade when they cross.
- **Sources:** [Double auction (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_auction), [arXiv: LOB as continuous double-sided auction](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.23784v1), [BSE "How to Trade"](http://www.bse.com.lb/GeneralInformation/Howtoinvest/tabid/73/Default.aspx).
- **Confidence:** Verified.

### S2. The busiest minute on Wall Street is an auction

### S4. Most IPOs are a hidden auction: book-building
- **Hook:** Did you know almost every IPO is priced by collecting bids, just not in public?

- **Why it matters:** **TEACH.** It contrasts closed bidding with open bidding.
- **60-sec angle:** "VIP-only auction vs open auction."
- **Sources:** [Investopedia, Book Building](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/b/bookbuilding.asp), [NBER WP 16214](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w16214/w16214.pdf).

### S5. The US government sells about $30 trillion of debt a year by auction, and you can bid from $100
- **Hook:** Did you know the US held 444 auctions in 2025 to borrow about $29.7 trillion?
- **Details:** All US Treasury marketable securities are sold at **public auctions**: in 2025, **444 auctions** and about **$29.7 trillion** issued. All winners pay the **same price** (uniform-price, or "Dutch", format; introduced for long-term securities in 1974). Individuals can bid **non-competitively** (up to $10 million per auction) via TreasuryDirect. The minimum bid has been **$100 since 2008**.

### W2. Babylon's bride auction (Herodotus)
- **Hook:** Did you know one of the oldest recorded auctions, described by Herodotus, was a marriage market in Babylon?
- **Details:** In *Histories* 1.196, Herodotus (5th c. BC) describes how once a year in each village, marriageable women were auctioned as wives, starting with the most beautiful. The money raised paid dowries for less-favoured brides.

### W3. The day Rome's empire was auctioned
- **Hook:** Did you know the Roman Empire was effectively sold to the highest bidder in 193 AD?
- **Details:** After the Praetorian Guard killed Emperor Pertinax (28 Mar 193), two men bid for the Guard's support: Sulpicianus offered 20,000 sesterces per soldier, and **Didius Julianus** won with **25,000 sesterces per soldier**. He reigned about **66 days**. The senator-historian Cassius Dio wrote that the empire was sold "as if in a market or auction room". ## D. PROBLEMS AND FIXES (owner, 23 Oct 2026: target customers' problems, give solutions, change their perspective)

*Compiled 23 October 2026 for the problem → reframe → fix lessons (see `LESSONS_SERIES.md`, "The strategy"). Each item is a buyer's or seller's real problem, the research that changes how they see it, and the auction mechanism that fixes it. The "mirrors" line is internal only: it names the app feature the fix prepares people for. Never put it on screen or in a caption.*

### P1. The first number wins: why lowball offers work
- **Problem:** Sami posts his car; the first message is a lowball. Haggling feels like a fight he can only lose.
- **Research:** In three experiments, whichever side made the first offer got the better deal, and first offers were a strong predictor of the final price (Galinsky & Mussweiler, *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 81(4), 2001). A later review: first offers often explain **over 50% of the variance** in final outcomes; Lipp (2022) reports a **.85 correlation** between first offer and final price in that study. Even professionals are pulled: real-estate agents' value estimates moved with the list price they were shown, and they **denied** being influenced (Northcraft & Neale, 1987).
- **Reframe:** A lowball isn't an insult; it's an anchor, and it works.
- **Fix:** In an auction, buyers name the numbers, and every new number is higher than the last.
- **Mirrors:** live bidding.
- **Sources:** [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11642352), [Columbia review of first offers](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/11691/first_offers.pdf), [Lipp 2022](https://ncmr.lps.library.cmu.edu/article/id/574), [Australian PM&C on Northcraft & Neale](https://www.pmc.gov.au/news/anchors-away-prices-anchors-and-auctions).
- **Confidence:** Verified (abstracts and reviews). Say "a strong predictor" or "over half"; don't say "decides the price".

### P2. One more buyer beats the best negotiator
- **Problem:** Selling to one buyer at a time: every chat starts again from zero.
- **Research:** Bulow & Klemperer, "Auctions versus Negotiations", *American Economic Review* 86(1), 1996: under standard assumptions a simple public auction is always more profitable than the best-structured negotiation, "provided that it attracts at least one extra bidder"; "the value of negotiating skill is small relative to the value of additional competition."
- **Reframe:** Your price problem isn't your negotiating; it's that only one buyer sees it at a time.
- **Fix:** Put every interested buyer in the same place at the same time.
- **Mirrors:** a public feed of live auctions.
- **Sources:** [Klemperer's abstract (Nuffield)](https://www.nuffield.ox.ac.uk/economics/papers/abstracts/klempc.htm), [IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/aea/aecrev/v86y1996i1p180-94.html).
- **Confidence:** Verified. It's a theory result "under standard assumptions"; present it as what economists proved, not a guarantee.

### P3. No deadline, no decision
- **Problem:** "Is it still available?" Days of chats, maybes, "I'll think about it".
- **Research:** Roth, Murnighan & Schoumaker, *AER* 78(4), 1988: in four bargaining experiments with a 9-minute clock on screen, **41% of agreements came in the last 30 seconds**, and **53% of those in the last 5 seconds** ("a striking concentration of agreements reached in the very last seconds before the deadline").
- **Reframe:** People don't decide when they're ready; they decide when time runs out.
- **Fix:** A countdown everyone can see.
- **Mirrors:** the auction countdown.
- **Sources:** [the paper (Stanford)](https://web.stanford.edu/~alroth/papers/1988_AER_Deadline_Effect.pdf).
- **Confidence:** Verified (lab experiments; say "in experiments").

### P4. The last-second steal, and the clock that fixes it
- **Problem:** Nour leads for days, then someone bids at the very last second and she has no time to answer.
- **Research:** Roth & Ockenfels (*AER* 2002) compared eBay (fixed end time) with Amazon's auctions (automatically extended until 10 minutes pass without a bid). In their eBay sample, **89 of 240 auctions had bids in the last minute and 29 in the last ten seconds**; on Amazon **only one bid** came in the last minute. 20% of last bids on eBay came in the last hour vs 7% on Amazon. Roth (HBS interview, 2005): 37% of the eBay auctions had bids in the last minute, 12% in the last 10 seconds.
- **Reframe:** A last-second win isn't skill; it's a rule that gives nobody time to answer.
- **Fix:** A late bid adds time, so every bid can be answered.
- **Mirrors:** "time added".
- **Sources:** [the paper (Stanford)](https://stanford.edu/%7Ealroth/papers/eBay.veryshortaer.pdf), [HBS Working Knowledge](https://hbswk.hbs.edu/item/amazon-ebay-and-the-bidding-wars).
- **Confidence:** Verified (2000-era data; say "in a study of eBay and Amazon auctions"). Never use the word "snipe/sniping" on screen or in captions (PLAYBOOK bans war words): say "last-second bids".

### P5. The no-show: why a deposit changes behaviour
- **Problem:** "I'm coming at 5." Nobody comes.
- **Research:** A booking platform's own data, Dec 2023 to Mar 2024: bookings with **full prepayment had a 0.9% no-show rate; with a deposit 1.7%; with a card hold 3%** (Tock). Lebanese law uses the same idea: to bid in a court auction you first deposit the full opening price, and losing bidders get it back (CCP Art. 973; item L1).
- **Reframe:** Serious people don't mind putting something down; time-wasters do.
- **Fix:** A small amount held when you bid, returned if you're outbid.
- **Mirrors:** the held amount on bids and listings (never state the amount).
- **Sources:** [Tock](https://www.exploretock.com/join/resources/eliminate-no-shows-3-tock-tools/), item L1.
- **Confidence:** Partly verified (company data; attribute it: "one booking platform found…").

### P6. Reputation is worth 8%
- **Problem:** Nour doesn't know the seller. Why trust a stranger?
- **Research:** Resnick, Zeckhauser, Swanson & Lockwood, *Experimental Economics* 2006, the first randomised field experiment on a reputation system: a high-reputation eBay dealer sold matched pairs of vintage-postcard lots under his own name and under brand-new seller names. Buyers paid **8.1% more** to the established name.
- **Reframe:** Trust isn't a feeling; it's a track record you can see.
- **Fix:** Public reviews and visible seller levels.
- **Mirrors:** trust tiers and public reviews (never say every seller is verified).
- **Sources:** [Resnick's project page](https://presnick.people.si.umich.edu/papers/postcards/index.html), [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/experimental-economics/article/abs/value-of-reputation-on-ebay-a-controlled-experiment/610EA11BA19583907061AFB677BE4338).
- **Confidence:** Verified (an early press release said 7.6%; the published paper says 8.1%; use 8.1%).

### P7. Paying a stranger first
- **Problem:** Online, someone has to pay before they see the item, and that's where scams live.
- **Research:** US FTC: consumers reported losing **$12.5 billion** to fraud in 2024; **online shopping was the second most reported fraud category**. In 2025, 3 million reports and **$15.9 billion** lost. Lebanon: on 9 Jul 2026 the ISF announced the arrest of a man who offered electrical appliances on social media, took payment, never delivered and refused refunds.
- **Reframe:** The risk isn't buying online; it's buying with no record, no rules and no one watching.
- **Fix:** Public track records plus money on the line from both sides. Never say "safe", "guaranteed" or "risk-free" (PLAYBOOK), and never claim the sale is protected: the sale is completed between buyer and seller.
- **Mirrors:** trust tiers, reviews, holds.
- **Sources:** [FTC 2025 release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/03/new-ftc-data-show-big-jump-reported-losses-fraud-125-billion-2024), [FTC 2026 testimony](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/03/ftc-testifies-joint-economic-committee-agencys-efforts-combat-fraud), [ISF statement](https://isf.gov.lb/news/suspect-arrested-for-fraudulent-sales-of-electrical-appliances-through-social-media-have-you-been-one-of-his-victims).
- **Confidence:** Verified. Never show or name the suspect.

### P8. Australia sells homes by auction every weekend
- **Problem (perspective):** In Lebanon, "auction" sounds like trouble: courts, banks, seizures.
- **Research:** Cotality (formerly CoreLogic): **1,749 homes** went to auction across Australia's capital cities in the week ending 11 Oct 2026 (Melbourne 703, Sydney 632); **2,871** in the week ending 15 Mar 2026. "Auctions are a popular way of selling houses in Australia, particularly in… Sydney and Melbourne" (Bloomberg). Note: 2026 clearance rates were weak (under 50% in October); don't claim auctions always sell.
- **Reframe:** Somewhere else, "auction" just means Saturday.
- **Fix:** n/a (perspective lesson).
- **Sources:** [Cotality preview, 18 Oct 2026](https://discover.cotality.com/hubfs/Article-Reports/Finalised%20clearance%20rates%20and%20auction%20market%20preview%2018%20October%202026.pdf), [Cotality, 16 Mar 2026](https://www.cotality.com/au/press-releases/strong-listings-activity-driving-auctions-up-but-clearance-rates-fading), [Business Times/Bloomberg](https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/property/australia-home-auction-clearance-rate-stuck-below-50-second-week).
- **Confidence:** Verified.

### P9. Melbourne made the price public (Oct 2026)
- **Problem:** "DM for price"; buyers never know the real number, or turn up and find it was never in their range.
- **Research:** Victoria, from 1 Oct 2026 (auctions from 16 Oct 2026): agents must publish the seller's **reserve price at least 7 days before** an auction, as a single dollar amount (no "from", "over", "+"); if it isn't published in time, **the auction can't go ahead**; the **sold price** must be published within 7 days of an unconditional sale and stay public for **18 months**. The minister: the purpose is "to end the frustration that prospective buyers feel when they go to an auction and learn that the house was never in their price range."
- **Reframe:** Hidden prices aren't a tactic; they're a cost, and some places now ban them.
- **Fix:** Bids in the open: everyone sees the same number at the same time.
- **Mirrors:** visible live bids; "reserve met / not met".
- **Sources:** [Consumer Affairs Victoria: the changes](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/licensing-and-registration/estate-agents/new-changes-to-property-sales-and-underquoting-laws), [CAV: selling by auction](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/buying-and-selling-property/selling-property/selling-property-by-auction), [Hansard, 14 Aug 2026](https://www.parliament.vic.gov.au/parliamentary-activity/hansard/hansard-details/HANSARD-974425065-36367).
- **Confidence:** Verified (official). Don't comment on whether the law is good for sellers.

### P10. The reserve price: a floor, not a ceiling
- **Problem:** Sami: "What if it sells for nothing?"
- **Research:** "The lowest price a seller is prepared to accept for their property at auction… is called the seller's reserve price." If bids don't reach it, the property is "passed in" and the highest bidder gets the first right to negotiate (Consumer Affairs Victoria).
- **Reframe:** An auction doesn't mean "any price"; the seller sets the floor and the buyers set the rest.
- **Fix:** A reserve.
- **Mirrors:** "reserve met / reserve not met".
- **Sources:** [CAV: selling by auction](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/buying-and-selling-property/selling-property/selling-property-by-auction).
- **Confidence:** Verified.

### P11. Japan's used cars go through auctions
- **Problem (perspective):** "Auctions are for art, or for seized things."
- **Research:** USS, Japan's largest car-auction group, FY2025 (Apr 2025–Mar 2026): a record **3.5 million vehicles** consigned, 67% sold; the whole Japanese car-auction market about **8 million vehicles** consigned; its Tokyo site handled **more than 20,000 vehicles in single auctions** in March and April 2026.
- **Reframe:** One of the world's biggest car markets prices ordinary used cars by auction, every week.
- **Sources:** [USS results script, May 2026](https://www.ussnet.co.jp/en/ir/item/script2026_4q.pdf), [USS auction data, Apr 2026](https://finance-frontend-pc-dist.west.edge.storage-yahoo.jp/disclosure/20260406/20260406598760.pdf).
- **Confidence:** Verified (company IR). These are dealer auctions; say "dealers", not "everyone".

### P12. You never pay much more than the runner-up
- **Problem:** Nour fears auctions make her overpay.
- **Research:** In an open ascending auction, bidders stop when the price passes what the item is worth to them, so the winner pays about **what the second-highest bidder was willing to pay, plus one step** (Vickrey 1961; Lucking-Reiley, *JEP* 2000; EconPort).
- **Reframe:** You don't pay what you're willing to pay; you pay one step above the last person who wanted it.
- **Fix:** Decide your maximum; the bidding stops when the last rival stops (callback to lesson 07).
- **Sources:** [Lucking-Reiley 2000](http://www.davidreiley.com/papers/VickreyHistory.pdf), [EconPort: English auction](https://www.econport.org/econport/request?page=man_auctions_englishauction).
- **Confidence:** Verified as theory (private values). Say "about" and "one step".

### P13. We pay extra just to avoid haggling
- **Problem:** Most people hate haggling: it's awkward, slow, and feels like a fight.
- **Research:** Hunsaker, Zhang & Lee (*Negotiation and Conflict Management Research*; Indiana University, 19 Dec 2025): across five studies, "95% of individuals choose not to negotiate up to 51% of the time"; people needed savings of **21–36%** of the price before negotiating felt worth it; businesses can raise prices **5–11%** and more than half of consumers will pay it. Consumer Reports (2016): 70% of used-car buyers haggled, succeeded 83% of the time, median saving **$900 (8%)** below the asking price.
- **Reframe:** Haggling has a price, and both sides pay it.
- **Fix:** Bidding replaces the back-and-forth with open rules.
- **Sources:** [Newswise / Indiana University](https://www.newswise.com/articles/why-many-americans-avoid-negotiating-even-when-it-costs-them), [Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/used-car-buying/why-haggling-for-your-next-car-really-pays).
- **Confidence:** Partly verified (press release; quote it exactly).

### P14. Your best buyer doesn't live on your street
- **Problem:** Sami's buyers are whoever happens to see his post nearby.
- **Research:** Christie's: **81% of all bids were placed online** in 2024 (78% in 2023); **63% of new buyers** made their first purchase online in 2025. Lebanon's Ministry of Economy and Trade estimates the Lebanese diaspora at about **12 million** people.
- **Reframe:** Your buyer might be in Sydney, Paris or Dubai, and willing to pay more than anyone nearby.
- **Fix:** Live online bidding open to everyone.
- **Sources:** [Christie's 2024 results](https://press.christies.com/56b-global-sales-in-2024-christies-sold-highest-value-lot-of-the-year-private-sales-up-40-yoy/), [artnet, Apr 2026](https://news.artnet.com/market/online-auctions-continue-to-draw-in-first-time-art-buyers-as-sales-grow-2763806), [Ministry of Economy and Trade](https://www.economy.gov.lb/en/projects/e-commerce).
- **Confidence:** Verified (diaspora figure is the ministry's estimate: attribute it).

### P15. Your home is full of unsold money
- **Problem:** Things sit in cupboards "in case", losing value.
- **Research:** eBay's Recommerce Report (2021): the average American home has **36 unwanted items worth $3,675**. musicMagpie (2021): an older iPhone loses about **10% of its resale value in the first month** after a new model comes out, and **23% after three months**.
- **Reframe:** Unused things aren't savings; they're losing value every month.
- **Sources:** [Forbes on eBay's report](https://www.forbes.com/sites/joanverdon/2021/03/16/average-home-has-36-things-that-could-sell-on-ebay-report-says), [WalesOnline on musicMagpie](https://www.walesonline.co.uk/news/cost-of-living/average-household-sitting-842-unused-25124999).
- **Confidence:** Partly verified (company surveys; attribute them; US/UK data, not Lebanon).

### P16. £45 in 1958, $450.3 million in 2017
- **Problem:** Sami prices things by guessing, and may give away what he doesn't know is valuable.
- **Research:** Leonardo's *Salvator Mundi* sold at Sotheby's in **1958 for £45**; it re-emerged in **2005**, bought from an American estate at a small regional auction house; on 15 Nov 2017 Christie's sold it for **$450,312,500 after 19 minutes** of bidding, at least 45 bids, after the auctioneer opened at $75 million (Christie's).
- **Reframe:** The seller didn't know what it was. Competition found out.
- **Fix:** Let the buyers set the price.
- **Sources:** [Christie's press release](https://press.christies.com/christies-sells-leonardo-da-vincis-500-year/), [Christie's story](https://www.christies.com/en/stories/leonardo-and-post-war-results-new-york-ad70dd5889e64989a76c94e4358c760d).
- **Confidence:** Verified. Never draw Christ's face or the painting itself: show a frame, a price tag and a clock.

### P17. Start low, end high
- **Problem:** Sami thinks a low starting price means a low final price.
- **Research:** Ku, Galinsky & Murnighan, *JPSP* 90(6), 2006, six studies: in auctions, **lower starting prices led to higher final prices**, because they bring more bidders, who then invest time and keep going. On real eBay data for **179 Persian rugs and 87 Nikon cameras**, a low start led to a higher final price, with more bids and more bidders.
- **Reframe:** In haggling, start high. In an auction, start low: the crowd does the rest.
- **Fix:** A low start plus a reserve (P10) protects the floor.
- **Sources:** [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16784346), [Kellogg Insight](https://insight.kellogg.northwestern.edu/article/place_your_bids).
- **Confidence:** Verified. Only works with traffic: the same paper shows a misspelled listing (few visitors) behaves like a normal anchor.

- **Sources:** [Ipsos](https://www.ipsos.com/en-lb/spotlightlebanon-online-shopper-behaviour-attitudes), [DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2024-lebanon).
