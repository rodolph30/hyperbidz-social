# Lesson 02 sources (opened and re-checked 17 Oct 2026)

- Cassius Dio, *Roman History*, Epitome of Book 74, 11–17 (Loeb translation, LacusCurtius): the empire "auctioned off" "as if… in some market or auction-room"; bids gradually raised to 20,000 sesterces per soldier; Julianus raised by 5,000 at once; reigned 66 days. https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Cassius_Dio/74*.html
- Wikipedia, "Didius Julianus": reign 28 March – 2 June 193; Sulpicianus 20,000 vs Julianus 25,000 sesterces per soldier; killed after 66 days. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didius_Julianus
- TheCollector, "The Day the Praetorians Sold the Roman Empire" (Kieren Johns, Aug 2026): 25,000 sesterces per guard, 66 days; the "auction" as escalating donatives. https://www.thecollector.com/praetorian-guard-sold-roman-empire
- Wiktionary, "auction": from Latin *auctio* ("an increase, auction"), from *augere* ("to increase"). https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/auction
- The $40 → $70 prices in the English-auction demo (Sami's radio) are cartoon illustrations, not real data.
