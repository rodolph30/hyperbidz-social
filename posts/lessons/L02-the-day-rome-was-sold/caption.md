# Lesson 02 · The day Rome was sold (the English auction)

## Option 1
In 193 AD, Rome's empire was sold at an auction. 🏛️

It worked like an English auction: bids only go up, and the last bid wins.
The emperor's guard ran the sale. Two rich men bid against each other while soldiers carried offers between them.
One offered 20,000 sesterces per soldier. The other jumped straight to 25,000, and won.
66 days later, he was overthrown.
Even the word "auction" comes from the Latin augere: to increase.

Source: Cassius Dio, Roman History, Book 74.

## Option 2
Rome was once sold to the highest bidder. 🤯

An English auction is simple: bids only go up, and the last bid wins.
In 193 AD, the guard put the throne up for bids. The winning offer: 25,000 sesterces per soldier.
The winner lasted 66 days.

Source: Cassius Dio, Roman History, Book 74.
