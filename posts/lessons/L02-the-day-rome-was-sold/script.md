## Facts (all re-checked 17 Oct 2026)
- After the guard killed Pertinax (28 Mar 193), the throne was put up to the highest bidder; Sulpicianus (inside the camp) and Didius Julianus (outside) bid against each other, soldiers carrying offers between them. Cassius Dio: "just as if it had been in some market or auction-room, both the City and its entire empire were auctioned off."
- Bids rose gradually to 20,000 sesterces per soldier (Sulpicianus); Julianus jumped by 5,000 at once to 25,000 and won.
- Julianus reigned 66 days and was killed in the palace on 2 June 193 as Septimius Severus took Rome ("overthrown").
- "Auction" comes from Latin *auctio*, from *augere*, "to increase".
- Some historians read the "auction" as Dio's description of escalating donatives (bribes); the script follows the ancient account, as the sources do.
