# Sources (lesson 26, re-checked 10 Nov 2026)

- Ku, G., Galinsky, A. D., & Murnighan, J. K. (2006). "Starting low but ending high: a reversal of the anchoring effect in auctions." J Pers Soc Psychol 90(6):975-86. Abstract: "6 studies ... document how and why, in the social setting of auctions, lower starting prices result in higher final prices ... barriers to entry that limit traffic (e.g., a misspelled brand name) lead to anchoring's normal assimilative effect rather than its reversal." https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16784346
- Kellogg Insight, "Place Your Bids" (Nov 2007): "Data including the starting and ending prices and the numbers of bids and bidders were analyzed from auctions for 179 Tabriz Persian rugs and 87 Nikon digital cameras. For both products, the researchers found that a low starting price led to a higher final price. They also verified that lower starting prices led to more bids and more unique bidders"; for misspelled listings, "low starting prices led to low ending prices." https://insight.kellogg.northwestern.edu/article/place_your_bids
