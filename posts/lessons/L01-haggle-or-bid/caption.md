# Lesson 01 · Haggle or bid? (price discovery)

## Option 1
In Lebanon, we love to haggle. But haggling quietly costs sellers money. 🤝

When you haggle, there's one buyer, one seller, and the price only goes one way: down.
Economists call the fix "price discovery": a price isn't really known until people compete for it. That's what an auction does.
True story: in 1995, a broken laser pointer sold for $14.83 on a tiny auction website. The buyer collected broken laser pointers. That website became eBay.
You don't know what your things are worth. The buyers do.

Source: eBay company history (AuctionWeb, September 1995).

## Option 2
Haggle or bid? 🤔

Haggling only moves the price one way: down.
An auction lets buyers compete, and that reveals what something is really worth.
Proof: the first thing ever sold on eBay was a broken laser pointer. $14.83, to a collector of broken laser pointers.
