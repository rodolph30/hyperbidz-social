# Caption (lesson 27)

## Option 1

A crier still has a job in Lebanese law. 📣

When a court sells seized goods, the officer may call in "the municipality's crier" (Code of Civil Procedure, Art. 930).
When a property goes to auction, the notice runs in two daily newspapers and the Official Gazette, and is posted on the municipality's door, at least ten days before (Arts. 970–971).
Why all that noise? Because a price is only fair when everyone can bid.
Online, the crier's job becomes a countdown everyone can watch, from anywhere.

Source: Lebanese Code of Civil Procedure, Arts. 930, 970–971 (legiliban, Lebanese University). The countdown screen and avatars in the video are illustrative.
An auction is only as fair as it is public. Did you know Lebanese law still mentions the crier? 👇

## Option 2

An auction is only as fair as it is public. 📣

Lebanese law still lets a court officer call in the municipality's crier to auction seized goods, and announces property auctions in newspapers, the Official Gazette and on the municipality's door, ten days ahead.
The crier's job was always the same: make sure everyone can bid.
