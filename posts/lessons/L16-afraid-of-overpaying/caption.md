# Lesson 16 captions

## Option 1
Scared you'll overpay at an auction? 😬

Nour was ready to go up to $700 for a bike.
The other bidders stopped at $450 and $500. She won at $510.
In an open auction, each bidder drops out at their limit, so the winner pays about one step above the runner-up, not her own limit.
Stamp auctions were already promising this in 1893: "one to ten cents above the second highest bidder."
Set your maximum before it starts, and let the others stop first.

Source: Lucking-Reiley, "Vickrey Auctions in Practice", Journal of Economic Perspectives (2000). The bike, bids and names in the video are illustrative.

## Option 2
You don't pay your limit. You pay one step above the last person who wanted it. 🚲

In an open auction, bidders drop out when the price passes their limit. The winner pays about the runner-up's limit, plus one step.

Source: Lucking-Reiley, Journal of Economic Perspectives (2000).
