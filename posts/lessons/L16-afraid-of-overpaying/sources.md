# Lesson 16 sources (re-checked 31 Oct 2026)

- Lucking-Reiley, D. (2000). "Vickrey Auctions in Practice: From Nineteenth-Century Philately to Twenty-First-Century E-Commerce." Journal of Economic Perspectives 14(3). On the English auction (Vickrey 1961): "each bidder's dominant strategy is to keep bidding until the price reaches her maximum willingness to pay for the good, and then to drop out of the auction. The highest-value bidder then ends up winning the good, and paying a price equal to the second-highest bidder's value (plus one bid increment, to guarantee victory)." The 1893 Wainwright & Lewis (Northampton, Mass.) sale notice: "each lot will be sold at an advance of from 1c to 10c above the second highest bidder."
  - http://www.davidreiley.com/papers/VickreyHistory.pdf
- EconPort, "English Auction": "The next-to-last person will drop out when her value is reached, so the person with the highest value will win at a price equal to the second-highest value."
  - https://www.econport.org/econport/request?page=man_auctions_englishauction
