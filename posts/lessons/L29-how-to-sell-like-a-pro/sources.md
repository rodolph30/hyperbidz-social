# Sources (lesson 29, re-checked 13 Nov 2026)

- Rule 1. Ku, Galinsky & Murnighan (2006), "Starting low but ending high: a reversal of the anchoring effect in auctions", J Pers Soc Psychol 90(6). Kellogg Insight, "Place Your Bids" (Nov 2007): "Data including the starting and ending prices and the numbers of bids and bidders were analyzed from auctions for 179 Tabriz Persian rugs and 87 Nikon digital cameras. For both products, the researchers found that a low starting price led to a higher final price. They also verified that lower starting prices led to more bids and more unique bidders." https://insight.kellogg.northwestern.edu/article/place_your_bids · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16784346
- Rule 2. Consumer Affairs Victoria, "Selling property by auction": "The lowest price a seller is prepared to accept for their property at auction ... is called the seller's reserve price"; "If bids do not meet your reserve ... the property will be 'passed in'." https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/buying-and-selling-property/selling-property/selling-property-by-auction
- Rule 3. Bulow & Klemperer (1996), "Auctions versus Negotiations", American Economic Review 86(1): "the public auction is always preferable, even if it forfeits all the seller's negotiating power ... provided that it attracts at least one extra bidder"; "the value of negotiating skill is small relative to the value of additional competition." https://www.nuffield.ox.ac.uk/economics/papers/abstracts/klempc.htm
- Rule 4. Roth, Murnighan & Schoumaker (1988), "The Deadline Effect in Bargaining: Some Experimental Evidence", American Economic Review 78(4): "Across all four experiments, 41 percent of the observed agreements were reached in the last 30 seconds of bargaining." https://web.stanford.edu/~alroth/papers/1988_AER_Deadline_Effect.pdf
