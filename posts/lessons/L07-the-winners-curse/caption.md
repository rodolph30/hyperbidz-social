## Option 1
A jar of coins worth $8. The winners paid $10.01. 🫙

In a classic experiment, MBA students at Boston University bid on jars of coins in 48 sealed-bid auctions. Every jar was worth $8.
Their average guess was only $5.13, yet the average winning bid was $10.01.
Why? The winner is usually the person who guessed highest, so winning often means you overestimated.
Oil engineers named it in 1971: the winner's curse. "If one won against 50 others, how should he feel? Ill."
The fix: decide your maximum before the bidding starts.

Sources: Bazerman & Samuelson, Journal of Conflict Resolution (1983); Capen, Clapp & Campbell, Journal of Petroleum Technology (1971); Thaler, Journal of Economic Perspectives (1988).
