# Lesson 07 sources (opened and re-checked 21 Oct 2026)

- Max H. Bazerman & William F. Samuelson, "I Won the Auction but Don't Want the Prize", Journal of Conflict Resolution 27(4), Dec 1983, pp. 618–634: subjects were M.B.A. students (N = 419) in 12 microeconomics classes at Boston University; 48 sealed-bid auctions; unknown to the subjects, every commodity was worth $8.00 (800 pennies, 160 nickels, paper clips); mean value estimate $5.13; "The mean AWB for the 48 auctions was $10.01 … Thus the average auction resulted in a loss of $2.01 to the winning bidder." https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/spr09/cos444/papers/BazermanSamuelson83.pdf
- Richard H. Thaler, "Anomalies: The Winner's Curse", Journal of Economic Perspectives 2(1), 1988, pp. 191–202: "The objects auctioned off were jars of coins or other objects such as paper clips … Unknown to the subjects, each jar had a value of $8"; the winner's curse "was first discussed in the literature by three Atlantic Richfield engineers, Capen, Clapp, and Campbell (1971)", about oil companies bidding for drilling rights; quotes Capen et al. (p. 645): "If one wins a tract against two or three others he may feel fine about his good fortune. But how should he feel if he won against 50 others?" "Ill." https://pubs.aeaweb.org/doi/pdf/10.1257/jep.2.1.191
- E. C. Capen, R. V. Clapp & W. M. Campbell, "Competitive Bidding in High-Risk Situations", Journal of Petroleum Technology 23, June 1971, pp. 641–653 (cited via Thaler and Bazerman & Samuelson). https://faculty.econ.ucsb.edu/~tedb/Courses/GameTheory/capen_et_al71.pdf
