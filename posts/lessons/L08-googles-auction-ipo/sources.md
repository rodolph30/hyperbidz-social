# Lesson 08 sources (opened and re-checked 22 Oct 2026)

- Los Angeles Times, "Wall Street May Have Taken Google to School", 19 Aug 2004: Google and some insiders "sold a total of 19.6 million shares at $85 each, down from the range of $108 to $135 a share originally estimated by Google." https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2004-aug-19-fi-ipo19-story.html
- CNN Money, "Google jumps in rocky debut", 19 Aug 2004: Google "closed its controversial Dutch auction"; first day: opened at $100, "closed at $100.34", 17–18% above the $85 offering price. https://www.cnn.com/2004/BUSINESS/08/19/google.ipo.close/index.html
- The Washington Times, "Google IPO raises $1.67 billion; shares rise 18 percent", 19 Aug 2004: raised $1.67 billion; the Dutch auction "allowed investors to submit bids for shares over the Internet, by fax and by telephone". https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2004/aug/19/20040819-111614-7454r
- "Going Dutch: The Google IPO", Berkeley Technology Law Journal 20 (2005): "a Dutch auction theoretically allows any investor to enter a bid"; "investors were required to order at least five shares"; 19,605,052 shares, $85, $1.67 billion; a modified Dutch auction. https://lawcat.berkeley.edu/record/1119854/files/fulltext.pdf
- The New York Times, "Weak Demand Leads Google to Lower Its Sights", 19 Aug 2004: shares cut to 19.6 million from 25.7 million. https://www.nytimes.com/2004/08/19/business/weak-demand-leads-google-to-lower-its-sights.html
- Investopedia, "Book Building": "An underwriter, usually an investment bank, invites institutional investors to bid on shares." https://www.investopedia.com/terms/b/bookbuilding.asp
